Fact-Checked Version — "Game Design Is System Design"

Source identification: This outline's structure (parts, chapters, even sub-bullets) matches Michael Sellers, Advanced Game Design: A Systems Approach (Addison-Wesley/New Riders, 2017) — confirmed against the publisher's table of contents. Recommendation: credit Sellers by name wherever this framework is presented, including in the video's on-screen text/description. See claim-ledger.md for full source-by-source verification.

Corrections applied

  1. Yerkes-Dodson framing (Ch. 4, flow discussion). Original: presented as settled law bookending the flow channel. Change: present as a popular, widely-cited-but-debated heuristic — a 2024 Trends in Cognitive Sciences paper explicitly revisits and critiques the "inverted-U" generalization beyond its original 1908 animal-study context. Flow theory itself (challenge ≈ skill) is well supported independently and doesn't need Yerkes-Dodson to stand.

  2. Quantic Foundry "mutually exclusive" claim (Ch. 4, interactivity budget). Original: "strategy games and excitement games are mutually exclusive." Change: "Quantic Foundry's player-motivation research shows strategy-seeking and excitement-seeking as negatively correlated preferences" — the data supports a correlation among player preferences, not a hard design exclusivity rule.

  3. "Michigan Fish Test" (Ch. 1). Minor caveat, no change to substance: this is the popular nickname for Takahiko Masuda & Richard Nisbett's cross-cultural perception research at Michigan, not the study's formal academic title. Fine to use as-is in an explainer context; flagged for precision.

Everything else

All other major claims — the systems-theory lineage (Bertalanffy, Wiener, Forrester, Holland, Meadows, Capra, Maturana/Varela, Luhmann), reinforcing/balancing feedback loops, Lotka-Volterra, Bushnell's Law, the Dwarf Fortress cat/alcohol story, Ultima Online's faucet/drain economy problems, and the MDA/FBS frameworks — check out as accurately represented. Sellers' own original terminology (Systemic Model of Games, second-order design, interactivity budget, designer's loop, engines/economies/ecologies) is treated as his framework, not an outside claim to verify, and is represented faithfully relative to the book's known structure.

What this means for the video

The short focuses on the core, best-supported material — the systems definition, feedback loops, elegance/depth/breadth, the flow channel (framed as "moderate challenge feels best," not hung on Yerkes-Dodson), and the designer's loop — and skips the two claims flagged above (Quantic Foundry specifics, strict Yerkes-Dodson framing) rather than repeating them uncorrected in a fast-paced format with no room for nuance.
