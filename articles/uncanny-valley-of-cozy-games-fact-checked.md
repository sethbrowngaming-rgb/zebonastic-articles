# The Uncanny Valley of Cozy Games: What the Evidence Supports

![An original illustration: a cozy cottage with a price tag on its roof and a row of identical smiling villagers.](../images/hero_cottage_storefront.png)
*Original illustration, not taken from any game.*

## 1. Introduction

Cozy games are marketed as low-stakes refuges: no combat, few fail states, an emphasis on domestic routine and community. A recurring critique says that this comfort can feel *off*. The critique borrows a term from robotics, the uncanny valley, and stretches it across three areas: how the games look, what their systems ask players to do, and how their communities and storefronts behave.

This article asks one question: **which parts of that critique are supported by evidence, and which are metaphor?** An earlier draft of this article stated the critique with more confidence than its sources allowed. Thirty-three claims were re-checked; eleven were confirmed as written, seven needed correction, and fifteen were removed or rewritten. The full ledger is in the project folder (`research/claim_ledger.md`).

![Bar chart: 11 claims confirmed, 7 partly right and corrected, 15 unsupported and removed or reworded.](../charts/05_audit.png)

## 2. Context

**The original hypothesis.** Masahiro Mori, a robotics professor, proposed the idea in 1970 in a text titled *Bukimi no Tani*. His hypothesis: as a robot looks more human, observers respond more warmly, until it becomes *almost* human, at which point the response turns sharply negative before recovering for a fully human appearance.

**The hypothesis is contested.** Critics, as summarized on Wikipedia, argue it may be "a heterogeneous group of phenomena" rather than one effect. Kätsyri, Mäkäräinen and Takala (2017) found that semirealistic animated film characters were rated eerier than cartoonish or live-action ones. But the same study found that cartoonish characters received the highest strangeness ratings and lowest likability after statistical adjustment. Kätsyri, de Gelder and Takala (2019) tested controlled virtual faces and reported a "weak UV effect for CG faces," concluding that responses resembled an "uncanny slope" more than a valley.

![Schematic chart contrasting Mori's dip with a rising slope.](../charts/01_uncanny_curve.png)

**"Cozy" is a loose term.** GameDiscoverCo's analysis of Steam descriptions found that games grossing over $100,000 that describe themselves as cozy rose from 0.4% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2025. The analysis notes that the word has diluted as it spread, so "cozy games" is not a tightly bounded category.

![Bar chart: cozy keyword share 0.4% in 2022, 3.1% in 2025.](../charts/03_cozy_share.png)

## 3. Evidence

### 3.1 Visual dissonance

**Observation.** The perception studies above used film characters and controlled faces, not game characters. Nothing in them tests cozy games specifically.

![Three schematic faces: simple, stylized, semi-realistic.](../images/faces_spectrum.png)

The earlier draft claimed that stylized art "bypasses" uncanny reactions entirely and that players "routinely report" rejecting mid-budget 3D cozy games such as *Wylde Flowers*, *My Time at Portia* and *Everdream Valley*. The first claim is not supported by the research (cartoonish characters were rated strange in the 2017 study), and no source was found for the second. Both were removed.

**One documented case.** *inZOI*, a life simulation published by Krafton, entered Steam Early Access on 28 March 2025 on Unreal Engine 5 with photorealistic characters. It reportedly sold over one million copies in its first week. GameSpot's review, as summarized on Wikipedia, praised its visuals but described "sterility and lifelessness."

**Interpretation.** One reading is that near-realistic characters paired with repetitive behavior feel hollow. Another is that reviewers were reacting to shallow simulation depth rather than to anything perceptual. The available sources document the reaction but do not establish the reason.

### 3.2 Ludic and economic dissonance

**Theory.** Bettina Bódi (2024, *Replay*) proposes "cozy agency," a mode of play between "the hypermasculine drive to successfully overcome challenges" and "the inessentiality of player action typical of idle games." She draws on Anthony Giddens's *ontological security*, the stability people derive from "continuous, reliable, predictable, unthreatening" everyday sequences. Her framing is a duality: cozy games function as both "indoctrination into, and escapism from, anxieties caused by neoliberal ideology."

**Observation: the Animal Crossing loan.** In *Animal Crossing: New Horizons*, Tom Nook is founder and CEO of Nook Inc. and finances the player's house. According to Nookipedia, a community wiki, house stages cost 98,000 Bells at the start and rise to 2,498,000 for the basement.

![Bar chart of house stage prices in Bells.](../charts/04_home_loan.png)

The sources consulted do not document interest or a repayment deadline, so this article does not describe the loan as coercive. Ian Bogost (*Persuasive Games*, 2007, as quoted on Wikipedia) called Nook "a kind of condensation of the corporate bourgeoisie" in the early games; Katherine Isbister cites Nook as an example of a mentor character. The game also includes a turnip market, which appears across the series: prices follow one of four patterns and unsold turnips spoil the following Sunday.

The earlier draft also described the game as involving "clear-cutting" and "strip-mining ecosystems," and said *Story of Seasons* ties progress to corporate debt. No sources support either statement; only *New Horizons*'s addition of terraforming is documented. Both were removed.

**Interpretation.** The parallel between resource-gathering loops and economic activity is a reasonable reading. Whether players experience it as dissonance is untested in the sources found.

### 3.3 Social sanitization

The earlier draft claimed that villagers in early *Animal Crossing* games were abrasive while those in *New Horizons* are uniformly polite, and that critics call this "Hollow Mode." Neither the term nor the contrast survived checking. According to Nookipedia, the original games have six personality types, including cranky and snooty, and *New Horizons* has eight; there, cranky and snooty villagers are "arrogant and rude, but can behave wise and caring when the player gets to know them." Screen Rant headlines argue that villagers used to be more abrasive, but the full text was not accessible, so it is treated as unverified opinion. Whether *New Horizons* is less socially varied than its predecessors remains open.

The philosophical argument in the draft also needs re-attribution. The application of Kant's Beautiful and Sublime, and the phrase "domesticity without domestication," come from Susan Haarman (*Analog Game Studies*, 4 July 2025). Her subject is the tabletop RPG *Yazeba's Bed & Breakfast*, and her argument is that its cozy aesthetic may "pacify" queer play by nudging players toward harmony. This is one author's reading of one tabletop game, not evidence about digital NPCs.

## 4. Case studies

### 4.1 *Disney Dreamlight Valley*

**Verified facts.** Early Access began on 6 September 2022. Access required a Founder's Pack or Game Pass, and the game was planned to become free-to-play. In October 2023 Gameloft cancelled that plan, and the game launched on 5 December 2023 as a paid title at $39.99 (Gold Edition $69.99; expansion pass $29.99). Premium currency (Moonstones) can be earned in play or bought, and per Gameloft's help page buying it "is not required to enjoy the base game." At Early Access launch, PC Gamer reported a free Star Path and a premium track costing 2,500 or 4,100 Moonstones.

![Timeline: Early Access September 2022, free-to-play plan cancelled October 2023, paid launch December 2023.](../charts/06_ddv_timeline.png)

**Developer statement.** Gameloft said Moonstone purchases would "remain optional, fair," and that the game would stay paid "for the foreseeable future" (VGC's reporting).

**Analysis.** A game that began under a free-to-play plan and kept a premium-currency shop after moving to a paid model combines two commercial models. Whether that undermines the "sanctuary" feel is an interpretation, and one the sources do not settle.

**Removed from the earlier draft:** that Star Paths compel daily logins, that energy meters push players to pay, and that the game is a "financial Skinner box." The energy meter is documented as spent on actions and restored by resting, eating or sitting; no source shows it can be bought.

**Limitation.** Player-experience data was not found; only design documentation and reporting.

### 4.2 *Discounty*

**Verified facts.** Developed by Crinkle Cut Games and published by PQube, released in August 2025. The player manages a franchise supermarket in a fictional harbor town, working toward targets set by a parent corporation. Wikipedia summarizes the premise as corporate expansion's effect on a small community.

**Reception.** Generally positive (OpenCritic 76), but reviewers criticized shallow storytelling and underdeveloped corporate commentary, according to the Wikipedia summary of reviews.

**Analysis.** The earlier draft described the game as exposing "supply-chain exploitation" and "regional corporate monopolies." Those claims exceed the documented premise. It is an example of a cozy game *touching* economic themes, and critics judged the treatment shallow.

### 4.3 *Dredge*

**Verified facts.** Developed by Black Salt Games and published by Team17; released 30 March 2023. The daytime loop is fishing and upgrading; at night a panic meter tracks the player's sanity and supernatural events appear. The art director has said the team wanted to leave "much to the player's imagination." It sold over one million copies by October 2023.

**Analysis.** *Dredge* is a clear case of a calm loop paired with horror, but it is not established that the developers intended a critique of cozy games.

*A 2015 horror game titled* Uncanny Valley *(a night-guard game) was also in the draft; it is not a cozy game and no source connects it to cozy-game critique, so it was dropped.*

## 5. Counterarguments and alternative interpretations

**Strongest opposing view: the "uncanny" framing is a metaphor stretched past its evidence.** The perceptual research is contested even for faces, and it says nothing about economic mechanics or storefronts. Stardew Valley (50M+ copies, per ConcernedApe in February 2026) and *Animal Crossing: New Horizons* (50.29M as of 30 June 2026, per Nintendo's results) sold at very large scale. There is no evidence in the sources that this audience feels dissonance rather than comfort.

**Supporting evidence for the opposing view.**
- *Stardew Valley* has been read as *critiquing* capitalism: Paste's Steven Wright called it anti-capitalist, citing Joja Corporation, and creator Eric Barone said he wanted "real-world messages." Production loops in a cozy game are not, by themselves, endorsement.
- Isbister reads Tom Nook as a mentor rather than a predator.
- Gameloft's position is that spending is optional and the base game is complete without it.
- Bódi's own framing is a duality, escape as well as indoctrination.

**Weaknesses in this article's argument.**
- Extending a perceptual hypothesis to economics and social design is an analogy, not a testable claim.
- The visual evidence comes from film and face studies, not game characters.
- Several data points come from Wikipedia summaries or a community wiki (Tier 2 and Tier 3).
- No player-perception study of cozy games was found.

**Unresolved questions.** Do players report unease at monetization in cozy games more than in other genres? Is *New Horizons* measurably less socially varied than earlier entries? Does photorealism in life simulations produce eeriness or merely shallow-simulation disappointment?

## 6. Limitations

- Sources were gathered from web searches in one session; paywalled or robots-blocked pages (Variety, Screen Rant) could not be read.
- Sales figures mix lifetime totals with milestone floors and are not directly comparable.
- Nookipedia and Wikipedia are secondary or community sources; game values should be re-checked in-game.
- The cozy-keyword series has only two published points.
- "Cozy" has no agreed definition.

## 7. Conclusion

**What we know.**
- Mori's hypothesis exists and is contested; two studies of films and virtual faces produced mixed results, including a weak effect for computer-generated faces.
- Bódi's paper argues cozy games both soothe and reproduce neoliberal productivity themes.
- *Animal Crossing: New Horizons* has a corporate-run house loan whose stages rise to millions of Bells.
- *Disney Dreamlight Valley* moved from a planned free-to-play model to a paid one and retains a premium-currency shop, which Gameloft describes as optional.
- Cozy-labeled games and cozy-adjacent games sell at scale.

**What we think.**
- The evidence suggests that "uncanny" is best read as a metaphor for mismatches between a game's comfort framing and its systems, not as a validated perceptual effect.
- Design questions worth testing: whether varied, occasionally difficult NPCs raise perceived authenticity; whether non-extractive loops change how players describe rest.

**What we don't know.**
- Whether players experience these features as dissonance.
- Whether *New Horizons* villagers are less varied than earlier ones.
- Why reviewers found *inZOI* "sterile."
- Whether developers of *Dredge* or *Discounty* intended cozy-genre critique.

## 8. Corrections from the fact-check pass

The most significant changes from the earlier draft:
- "Domesticity without domestication" and the Kant framing are Haarman's, and she uses them to *question* a cozy game, not to praise it.
- *Discounty*'s themes were overstated; reviewers called its corporate commentary underdeveloped.
- *New Horizons* does include cranky and snooty villagers; "Hollow Mode" was removed for lack of a source.
- *Dreamlight Valley* claims about daily-login pressure, paid energy and "permanent" microtransactions were removed or softened.
- Unsourced claims about player reactions to *Wylde Flowers*, *My Time at Portia*, *Everdream Valley* and *inZOI* animation faults were removed.
