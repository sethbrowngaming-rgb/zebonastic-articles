# Fewer Consoles, More Games?

What Nintendo's and Sony's September-quarter reports do, and do not, show about the hardware business

21 November 2026 · Evidence-graded analysis · Claim ledger published alongside

*[Image cover.png: Title card reading Fewer Consoles, More Games? What Nintendo's and Sony's September-quarter reports do, and do not, show about hardware.]*

## 1. Introduction

In the first week of November 2026, Nintendo and Sony each reported results for the quarter that ended on 30 September. The headlines looked alike. Nintendo said Switch 2 hardware shipments fell 37.4% in April to September, while its Switch 2 software units rose 18.8%. Sony's PlayStation 5 shipped 2.6 million units in July to September against 4.0 million a year earlier, while Sony's software units and monthly active users rose. Days later, Sony's data page showed the PS5 had passed 100 million units shipped.

A tidy story fits those facts: players are buying fewer consoles and more games, so the business is moving from hardware to software. This article tests that story against the disclosures. The central question is narrow: **what do these reports establish about why hardware shipments fell, and do they show a shift from hardware to software?**

The short version of the findings: the reports establish how large the hardware decline is. They do not establish its cause. At Nintendo, software growth is smaller than growth in the installed base, so it does not demonstrate deeper engagement per player. The full evidence is below, with a [what we know, think and do not know](#know) summary at the end.

**How to read this.** Statements are labeled where it matters: Observation a measurable figure from a source; Interpretation a conclusion drawn from evidence, including our arithmetic. Source tiers: Tier 1 is company filings, presentations and data pages; Tier 2 is industry journalism. No Tier 3 or Tier 4 material is used as fact.

## 2. Context

### Two measures that are not the same as sales to players

Both companies report *sell-in*, meaning units sold or shipped into the sales channel, not units bought by players. Nintendo defines sell-in as sales by the group to outside parties, and that includes units sold through its own online stores. Sony's data page states that its hardware figures include returned and refurbished products. Neither company publishes sell-through. Every hardware figure in this article therefore describes shipments, and shipments can run ahead of or behind what players actually buy.

### Two consoles at different points in their lives

The Switch 2 launched on 5 June 2025, so the April to September 2025 comparison period contains its launch window. The PS5 launched in November 2020 and is in its sixth year. A year-over-year comparison for the Switch 2 is a comparison against a launch spike. For the PS5 it is a comparison against a mature year.

### Both companies raised prices

Observation: Sony raised US PS5 prices by about \$50 in August 2025, then by \$100 for the base models and \$150 for the PS5 Pro, effective 2 April 2026, which put the disc model at \$649.99 and the Digital Edition at \$599.99 (Yahoo Finance, Reuters). Sony's announcement cited “continued pressures in the global economic landscape”, as quoted by PC Gamer; Reuters tied the move to rising memory chip costs.

Observation: Nintendo announced Switch 2 price rises in early May 2026, citing “changes in market conditions” (Game Informer). In Japan the price rose on 25 May, from ¥49,980 to ¥59,980. In the US, Canada and Europe it rose on 1 September; the US price went from \$449.99 to \$499.99 (Video Games Chronicle, Gematsu).

## 3. Evidence

### Nintendo

Observation: Nintendo's six-month results, published on 5 November, put Switch 2 hardware sell-in at 6.49 million units for April to September, down 37.4% from the same period a year earlier. Switch 2 software was 24.49 million units, up 18.8%. Cumulative Switch 2 hardware stood at 26.35 million units on 30 September. Consolidated net sales fell 12.1% to ¥966.0 billion, which Nintendo attributed primarily to lower hardware unit sales; operating profit rose 49.1% to ¥216.5 billion (Nintendo presentation, Anadolu).

Observation: The July to September quarter alone was about 2.67 million units, against 4.54 million a year earlier (Anadolu Agency). A separate report gives 2.68 million; the difference is rounding in cumulative figures.

*[Image chart-switch2.png: Bar chart of Switch 2 units for April to September 2025 versus 2026. Hardware fell from 10.36 million to 6.49 million, down 37.4 percent. Software rose from 20.62 million to 24.49 million, up 18.8 percent. A note says the installed base grew from 10.36 million to 26.35 million, so software grew far less than the base.]*

*Figure 1. Switch 2 hardware and software units, April to September. Prior-year figures are derived from Nintendo's reported percentages and cumulative data.*

Observation: Nintendo left its full-year forecast unchanged: 16.5 million Switch 2 units for the fiscal year ending March 2027, set on 8 May 2026. After 6.49 million in the first half, that leaves about 10.01 million for October to March. The Switch 2 shipped 19.86 million units in the fiscal year ended March 2026 and 10.36 million in its first half, which implies about 9.50 million in last year's second half. Interpretation: The unchanged forecast therefore requires a second half roughly 5.4% larger than last year's, for a console that now costs more.

### Sony

Observation: Sony Interactive Entertainment's data page lists PS5 sell-in of 1.6 million units in April to June 2026 and 2.6 million in July to September, against 2.5 million and 4.0 million a year earlier. Fiscal 2025 sell-in was 16.0 million, down from 18.5 million in fiscal 2024. The cumulative total reached 97.9 million on 30 September (our sum of the annual and quarterly figures) and was listed as more than 100 million on 10 November.

*[Image chart-ps5-h1.png: Bar chart of PS5 sell-in for April to September in each fiscal year: 5.6 million in fiscal 2021, 5.7 in 2022, 8.2 in 2023, 6.2 in 2024, 6.5 in 2025 and 4.2 in 2026, a 35 percent fall from 2025.]*

*Figure 3. PS5 sell-in for April to September by fiscal year, in millions. Each bar is the sum of Sony's Q1 and Q2 figures.*

Observation: Sony's software units (PS4 and PS5 combined) were 88.2 million in July to September, 7.9 million more than a year earlier, and monthly active users were 127 million, an estimate based on SIE research, up 8 million on the year (SIE data page; Engadget via T-Break for the software change; Instant Gaming for the user change). Interpretation: Sony's quarter combined falling hardware with rising software and users. That is the pattern the tidy story describes. The next sections ask what produced it.

### What the disclosures cannot show

The available sources document the size of the changes but do not establish the reasons for them. Neither company publishes sell-through, so neither report shows whether players bought fewer consoles or retailers simply ordered fewer. Neither quarter includes the holiday period; in Sony's fiscal 2025, October to December alone accounted for 7.9 of 16.0 million units, about half the year.

## 4. Case studies

### Case 1: Switch 2, a decline measured against a launch

**Verified mechanics.** The comparison base contains the launch window. Nintendo reported 19.86 million Switch 2 units for the fiscal year ended March 2026, against an initial forecast of 15 million and a revised forecast of 19 million (Reuters; GamesBeat). The first half of that year, which includes the launch, accounts for 10.36 million of the total (KeenGamer, from Nintendo's report).

**Company statements.** In May, Nintendo said launch-year sales had been more concentrated than for earlier hardware, and its guidance for a year-over-year decline in fiscal 2027 units named both that concentration and the price revisions as reasons (GamesBeat; Nintendo Life; GamingBolt). In the November Q&A, as summarized in Nintendo's published Q&A document, Shuntaro Furukawa did not describe the half-year fall as caused by price. He said sell-through in Japan spiked ahead of the May price revision and then settled. Outside Japan, he said, the September revision had been announced well in advance and came with bundles, so trends before and after it differed less. He said higher-priced component procurement increases from the third quarter, so the cost effect is larger in the second half, and he called the next several weeks key to reaching guidance.

**Analysis.** Interpretation: Nintendo's own account points to two contributors: the launch concentration it forecast in May, and the price revisions it cited alongside it. The Japan pull-forward Furukawa described shows price changes moving purchases in time. The November materials reviewed do not apportion the half-year decline between the two. In the West, the rise took effect on 1 September, in the last month of the July to September quarter.

On the software side, the installed base at the end of the period grew from 10.36 million to 26.35 million units over the year, an increase of about 154%. Software units rose 18.8%. Interpretation: Software growing far more slowly than the base is consistent with fewer software units per console, not more. An alternative explanation is that the prior-year half contained software bundled with launch hardware, which Nintendo counts in its software units. The sources do not allow the two to be separated.

**Limits.** Nintendo's presentation does not explain the hardware decline. The company's operating profit rose 49.1% in the same period; the sources reviewed do not itemize why, so this article draws no conclusion from it.

### Case 2: PS5, a decline that predates the April price rise

**Verified mechanics.** Sony's quarterly table allows a year-over-year comparison for each quarter. Our arithmetic: April to June 2025 was +4% and July to September 2025 was +5%. Then October to December 2025 was −17%, January to March 2026 was −46%, April to June 2026 was −36%, and July to September 2026 was −35%.

*[Image chart-ps5-yoy.png: Bar chart of PS5 sell-in change versus the same quarter a year earlier. April to June 2025 plus 4.2 percent, July to September 2025 plus 5.3, October to December 2025 minus 16.8, January to March 2026 minus 46.4, April to June 2026 minus 36.0, July to September 2026 minus 35.0. Annotations mark the August 2025 price rise and the April 2026 price rise.]*

*Figure 4. PS5 sell-in versus the same quarter a year earlier. Percentages are our arithmetic on Sony's published quarterly figures.*

**Company statements.** Sony's chief financial officer told investors that new PS5 sales are “gradually declining as expected in this stage of the console life cycle”, as reported by God Is a Geek. The primary transcript was not available for this review.

**Analysis.** Interpretation: The data are consistent with a lifecycle decline that began in late 2025, before the April 2026 price rise took effect. They are also consistent with the August 2025 price rise, or the April 2026 rise, deepening a decline already under way. The step in pace is large, though: annual sell-in fell about 11% in fiscal 2024 and about 13.5% in fiscal 2025 (our arithmetic), while April to September 2026 is down 35%.

A base effect needs weighing too. The October to December 2024 quarter, Sony's largest in the table at 9.5 million units, contained the PS5 Pro launch on 7 November 2024. The 17% decline for October to December 2025 is measured against that peak.

**Limits.** Sony's CFO statement is a secondhand report. Sony's own materials do not state a cause for the shipment figures in the sources reviewed.

## 5. Counterarguments and alternative interpretations

### The strongest opposing view: this is a price story

The strongest case against a lifecycle reading is that both companies raised prices and shipments fell. Sony's year-over-year decline was −36% in the first quarter after the April increase took effect. Furukawa's own description of a Japan spike before the May increase shows that buyers respond to announced price changes, at least in timing. A US price of \$649.99 for the disc PS5 is \$100 above its price before April.

*Weaknesses of this view.* Sony's decline started in October to December 2025 and reached −46% in January to March 2026, before the April increase. In its November materials Nintendo did not apportion the half-year decline to price, and its May guidance named launch concentration alongside price. Without sell-through data, a fall in shipments cannot be assigned to buyers rather than to retailers' ordering. Price and lifecycle effects also overlap in time, so the sources cannot separate them.

### A second opposing view: the first half says little about the year

Both companies earn a large share of their hardware volume in the holiday quarter; for Sony's fiscal 2025 it was about half. If the weaker first half reflects retailers stocking later, or Nintendo's bundle-led holiday plan, the full-year picture could look different. Nintendo's unchanged forecast implies it expects that. *Weakness:* an unchanged forecast is a statement of expectation, not evidence; the 10.01 million required exceeds last year's second half.

### A third view: software growth proves the shift

Software units and active users rose at both companies, which the shift story reads as the business moving toward software. *Weakness:* installed bases also grew. Nintendo's base is up about 154% against software up 18.8%. Sony's software count includes PS4 titles and bundled games, and the sources do not give per-player figures.

### Unresolved questions

- How much of the shipment decline is sell-through and how much is channel ordering?
- Do price rises explain the pace of the decline, or only its timing?
- Do memory costs constrain how many consoles are built, as distinct from what they cost? Nintendo places the higher procurement costs from the third quarter, after the period covered here, and the Q&A summary reviewed does not cite supply limits.
- Does the holiday quarter confirm or contradict the first half?

## 6. Limitations

- Two companies, two consoles. Microsoft, Valve and PC hardware are not covered.
- Sell-in is not sell-through. Nintendo's figures include its own online sales; Sony's include returned and refurbished units.
- Nintendo's prior-year software and hardware figures are derived from its reported percentages and cumulative data, not read from a prior-year table.
- Nintendo's documents and Sony's data page were read through a fetch-and-summarize tool, not page by page. Sony's investor-relations site was not reachable, so Sony's earnings materials were not reviewed directly. The CFO's remark is secondhand.
- Fiscal years differ from calendar years. Both companies use an April to March year, which this article follows.
- Our arithmetic (percent changes, second-half requirement, base growth) is labeled as such and rests on rounded published figures.

## 7. Conclusion

### WHAT WE KNOW

- Switch 2 hardware shipments fell 37.4% in April to September against a launch-window comparison; software units rose 18.8% (Nintendo).
- PS5 shipments fell 35% in July to September, and the decline in each of the last three quarters was between 35% and 46% (Sony data, our arithmetic).
- Both companies raised prices in 2026, and both report sell-in, not sales to players.
- Nintendo's unchanged forecast needs a second half about 5.4% above last year's.

### WHAT WE THINK

- A lifecycle and launch-concentration effect is part of the Switch 2 decline, because Nintendo forecast it. The evidence suggests the PS5 decline began before the April price rise.
- Price rises may have contributed: Nintendo cited price revisions alongside launch concentration in its May guidance, and Japan shows purchases shifting around a price change. The evidence does not show how much.
- Software growth at Nintendo is smaller than installed-base growth, so it does not support a claim of deeper engagement per player.

### WHAT WE DON'T KNOW

- Why shipments fell. The available sources document the event but do not establish the reason.
- What players actually bought, because sell-through is not published.
- Whether the holiday quarter will confirm the trend.
- Whether the shift from hardware to software is real or a product of larger installed bases and bundles.
